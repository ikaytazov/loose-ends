1. Use case exactly as described in the meeting

A fictional firm has roughly 8–10 people: a CEO responsible for the overall plan, workstream owners responsible for parts of it, and team members who execute tasks. David will prepare 3–5 sequential weekly team-call transcripts and a corresponding team/workstream structure. A user uploads the transcripts through a simple landing page. The team is not expected to hold actual meetings for the demonstration. The uploaded records establish the tasks and their evolution over time. [06:36–08:34; 21:10–23:57]

The CEO uses the web interface to see how projects/workstreams and tasks are progressing. Each task member receives a morning email digest listing their own tasks and priorities, and replies naturally with progress, no progress, or a request for more time. The application checks its shared Gmail inbox approximately every minute, processes replies, and tries to update tasks. 4. Additional use cases worth testing (proposed, not agreed on the call)

Mixed reply: one email says task A is complete, task B is untouched, and task C needs an extension. Update each only when a reliable task match exists; queue the extension separately.

Unknown update: a member says “I'm working on it” without naming a task while they have three tasks. Ask which one; leave statuses unchanged.

Owner availability: the workstream owner is also the requester, absent, or not present in the fixture. Escalate to the CEO or an explicitly configured delegate; do not self-approve by default.

Changing dependency: Priya completes the figures after Alex reports a blocker. Show the new evidence; do not automatically mark Alex's deck complete.

Successive weekly calls: a later transcript revises or contradicts an earlier task. Preserve the original agreement and ask for review rather than overwrite silently.

Manual upload fallback: paste text if file conversion fails. Retain meeting title/date and show an error that allows retry without creating duplicate records.

Leadership review: CEO filters workstreams with decisions, blockers or no recent updates. “No update” means unknown, not on track.

Expected handling

Detect duplicate or offer explicit confirmation; never silently create duplicate tasks.

Explain the problem and accepted formats; preserve entered metadata and notes.

Transcript without speaker, owner or date

Flag missing fields for review; do not invent an owner or deadline.

Multiple people make the same commitment

Present separate proposals or ask for a named accountable owner.

Link to existing task or seek review of a new version.

Ask for clarification before mutating the ambiguous one.

No task mutation; record safe diagnostic/audit information.

Process once using message ID/idempotency key.

Hold or reject safely, then retry or seek manual match; never attach arbitrarily.

Clarify calendar-days versus working-days policy; keep approved date until decision.

One decision succeeds; later attempt sees already-decided state.

Owner approves after task completed or superseded

Recheck current version and ask for confirmation if stale.

Use configured timezone, store UTC event times, test the boundary.

Sensitive or real team email addresses in fixtures

8. Paste-ready addition to the product brief

Primary use case: Loose Ends supports a small company with a CEO, workstream owners and task executors. A lead uploads sequential weekly meeting transcripts and reviews the extracted tasks. Each morning, team members receive an email showing their own tasks and priorities. They reply naturally with progress, no progress or requests for more time. The application processes those replies and updates only confidently matched tasks. A request for more time is presented to the relevant workstream owner for approval or decline; the approved due date remains in force until that decision. The CEO sees workstream progress and outstanding attention items in the web interface. For the hackathon, simulated transcripts and an upload page establish the workflow, while automated meeting capture remains a later enhancement.

Example story walkthrough: extension request

Given Alex has an approved task due Wednesday and Priya is its workstream owner, when Alex replies to the digest, “I have not finished the deck; can I have three more days because I am waiting on pricing?”, then Loose Ends records the blocker/no-progress information and creates a pending date request for Priya. The approved Wednesday date remains visible. When Priya approves, the new due date is calculated under the agreed calendar rule, saved once and reflected in the CEO view. When Priya declines, Wednesday remains. The named people, date and dependency here are an illustrative scenario; David's actual fixture and the repository's due-date logic govern the demo.
